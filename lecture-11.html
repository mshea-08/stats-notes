11  More Confidence Intervals

11.1 Confidence Intervals for Proportions

If we know the sample proportion and want to compute a confidence interval for the true proportion we use the following formula for the interval,

\[\left(\hat{p} - z_{\text{CL}}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}, \bar{x} + z_{\text{CL}}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}\right)\]

The margin of error is the term that is added/subtract, e.g. 

\[z_{\text{CL}}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}\]

where \(z_{\text{CL}}\) is the appropriate \(z\)-score for our confidence interval.

In Topic 5, we were only allowed to assume the distribution of the sample proportion was normal if \(np \geq 10\) and \(n(1-p) \geq 10\). For the same reason, we cannot compute the confidence interval unless we have satisfied the conditions,

\[n\hat{p} \geq 10 \text{ and } n(1-\hat{p}) \geq 10.\]

Example 11.1 Jean randomly samples 200 nurses at her hospital and find that 82% of them commute to work by car. Construct a 90% confidence interval for the proportion of all nurses at Jean’s hospital who commute to work by car.

TipAnswer

11.1.1 Work by Hand

The sample proportion is \(\hat{p} = 0.82\). Since we are interested in a 90% confidence interval, we use \(z_{CL} = 1.645\). The margin of error is then,

\[z_{\text{CL}}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}} = 1.645 \times \sqrt{\frac{0.82(0.18)}{200}} = 0.0447.\]

So the confidence interval is \((0.82-0.0447, 0.82+0.0447) = (0.7753, 0.8647)\).

11.1.3 Summary Sentence

Example 11.2 A state’s department of education is interested in the proportion of all eighth-grade students in their state that will score at less-than-proficient in math on a national assessment. A random sample of 100 student were studied and it was found that \(\hat{p} = 0.58\). Construct a 95% confidence interval for the proportion of eighth-grade students at a less-than-proficient mathematics level.

11.1.4 Work by Hand

Since we are interested in a 90% confidence interval, we use \(z_{CL} = 1.96\). The margin of error is then,

\[z_{\text{CL}}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}} = 1.96 \times \sqrt{\frac{0.58(0.42)}{100}} = 0.0977.\]

So the confidence interval is \((0.4823, 0.6777)\).
